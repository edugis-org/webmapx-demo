## what

Two related things: **pitch**, which tilts the camera away from straight down,
and **terrain**, which gives the ground its actual relief instead of drawing it
flat.

Tilting alone changes how a map reads — a city seen at 60° shows its skyline
and its street canyons, and a valley shows that it is a valley. Terrain goes
further and deforms the surface itself from elevation data, so a mountain has a
back you cannot see over.

Pitch presets (0°, 30°, 60°) are offered because arriving at a useful angle by
dragging is fiddly, and because 0 is the one people most want back.

Not every engine does both, and the tool says which rather than offering
buttons that do nothing.

## use

1. Pick a pitch, or drag with the right mouse button (two fingers on a touch
   screen) for any angle in between.
2. Turn terrain on where the map has elevation data.
3. Return to 0° to get the plan view back.

A tilted map is worse for measuring and comparing — everything far away is
smaller — so tilt for reading the landscape, and flatten again for reading the
data.

## embed

Add `3d` to a toolbar. Terrain uses a `raster-dem` source already on the map;
where a map has none, a fallback can be configured:

- `maplibre-terrain-fallback-url`
- `cesium-terrain-fallback-url`

**The terrain outlives the panel.** Closing the tool does not switch it off —
closing is how you get the map to yourself, and taking the relief away at that
moment removes the very thing the panel was closed to look at.

## extend

`setTerrainEnabled` mirrors its result into the store only when the engine
reports the change applied, so `store.terrainEnabled` never claims a terrain
the map does not have. That field doubles as a command channel: a config or a
permalink asking for terrain dispatches it, and this tool reacts by enabling
terrain against the map's own hillshade layer.
